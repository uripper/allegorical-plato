# Background

Of the existent letters by Plato, the seventh letter is the only one academics broadly agree is written by him. But if Plato wrote the other 12, especially the second, he is very different than we make him out to be. Plato has a reputation among the public of being one of the most practical, easy to read philosophers, creating one of the best entry points for learning philosophy. Though the letters written by Plato, if they are in fact written by Plato, seem to imply that Plato was not attempting to write easy philosophy. He was instead doing something very different, something hidden.

Take the following quote:

> Now I must expound it to you in a riddling way in order that, should the tablet come to any harm in folds of ocean or of earth, he that readeth may not understand.
>
> *(Potentially) Plato, The Second Letter (312D)*

This is explicitly inviting what is referred to as the allegorical interpretation of Plato. Or at least an allegorical interpretation of Plato’s second letter. Throughout history, allegorical interpretations of Plato have weaned and waned. They were central to Neoplatonism, basically defining the entire thought of Plotinus. Now they’re fairly niche in modern Platonism.

But what does it mean to allegorically interpret Plato? To understand this, you need to understand an older philosopher that seems incompatible with Plato; Pythagoras.

> Number is the ruler of forms and ideas, and the cause of gods and daemons.
>
> *Pythagoras, quoted in Life of Pythagoras (c. 300) by Iamblichus of Chalcis, translated by Thomas Taylor (1818)*

Pythagoras is widely known today because of triangles, but anyone who has had a passing interest in Ancient Greek philosophy has heard him described as a cult leader and fanatic, obsessive over numbers. He also, reportedly, believed beans should not be eaten. This could have been a complicated metaphor for advising others not to get involved in politics, as voting was maybe done with beans. Or, it was because he believed beans had souls, which is more fun.

Much of his philosophy was a mathematical mysticism that understood the reality of objects existed behind their appearances. Most importantly, Pythagoras and his followers were believed to shroud their doctrines within symbols. This was to hide the teachings from those who they deemed unworthy or incapable of understanding. What would remain written and taken at face value would ultimately be structurally hollow.

> But thus much I can certainly declare concerning all these writers, or prospective writers, who claim to know the subjects which I seriously study, whether as hearers of mine or of other teachers, or from their own discoveries; it is impossible, in my judgement at least, that these men should understand anything about this subject. There does not exist, nor will there ever exist, any treatise of mine dealing therewith. For it does not at all admit of verbal expression like other studies, but, as a result of continued application to the subject itself and communion therewith, it is brought to birth in the soul on a sudden, as light that is kindled by a leaping spark, and thereafter it nourishes itself.
>
> *Plato, The Seventh Letter (341B-341D)*

So if Plato were to be a secret Pythagorean, he likely would have hidden it. Not just due to protecting what he believed to be the sanctity of his work, but also protecting himself. It’s reasonable he would be paranoid of being found impious, as Socrates was, or more so if Plato truly believed in Pythagorean mysticism.

# What this project aims to do

This project attempts to find recurring motifs, coded language, symbolism, etc… via the usage of modern machine learning techniques. This is meant to be a fun way to visualize a corpus of philosophy and find interesting or surprising connections. Even lacking any proof, there will almost certainly be fun explorations of data.

---

# Limitations

The current oldest Greek texts we have of Plato are copies from the Byzantine Empire from around 800-900 C.E. Plato died in 347 B.C.E. Needless to say, the words may not have been perfectly preserved over 1200 years of copying. This pretty significantly limits the possibility of finding allegorical proof in Plato if every single word was intentional and important to constructing the allegorical Plato.

Machine learning techniques also aren’t very likely to discover what codes and symbols existed in the heads of the Pythagoreans, nor Plato, if he were one.

There is also a strong possibility that, if certain patterns indicating repeated Pythagorean overtures are found, they may be completely spurious or invented in the process of me hoping to find something.

This is not an academic study, and would not stand up to peer review or academic rigor. I will do everything to make it as rigorous as possible, but this is a hobby project in my free time. Take every claim I make skeptically.
